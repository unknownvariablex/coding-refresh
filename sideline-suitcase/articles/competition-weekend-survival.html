Competition Weekend Survival Guide (From a Mom Who's Done Too Many)

By Kylee · Updated October 28, 2026

Two days, one arena, a 7 a.m. report time, and a sibling who needs a snack every 11 minutes. Here's how we make it work.

Short version: arrive the night before, prep hair at the hotel, build your day around the report time, feed everyone real food, and have a plan for the siblings.

The night before

  • Check in, steam the uniform, lay out everything in the order you'll need it.
  • Do as much hair prep as your gym allows.
  • Check the schedule one more time; times shift.
  • Plan parking. Pre-pay if the venue offers it.

Competition morning

  • Breakfast with protein, even if they're nervous.
  • Leave earlier than you think. Security, wristbands and long walks add up.
  • Know where warm-ups are versus where you'll sit to watch.

Saving money on the weekend

  • Pick a hotel with free breakfast and a fridge.
  • Grocery stop on the way in: water, fruit, sandwich supplies.
  • Carpool with another team family and split parking.
  • Earn hotel points on every stay (here's how I do it).

Keeping siblings happy

A dedicated sibling bag (tablet, headphones, snacks, a small treat they only get at comps), a break outside between rounds, and one "their choice" meal for the weekend.

Quick answers

How long does a cheer competition last?

Most all-star competitions run one or two days. Your athlete's team usually performs once per day, but report time, warm-ups and awards can take up most of a day.

What do parents need at a cheer competition?

A spectator wristband or ticket, a seat cushion, a light jacket, a phone battery, water and snacks if allowed, cash for parking, and patience for crowds.

How can I save money on cheer competition travel?

Book a hotel with free breakfast and a fridge, grocery shop on arrival, carpool with team families, and earn hotel points on each stay that you can use on a future trip.
