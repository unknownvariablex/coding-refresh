How I Turn Competition Season Into Free Hotel Nights

By Kylee · Updated October 28, 2026

Travel sports are expensive. Points don't make them free, but they take a real bite out of the hotel bill. Here's the simple system I use.

Short version: pick one hotel program, put your number on every stay, pay for travel with a card that earns in that program, pay the card off every month, and save points for the weekends that aren't stay-to-play.

Step 1: Pick one hotel family

Spreading stays across five chains means you never have enough points for anything. I picked Marriott Bonvoy because there's usually a Courtyard, Residence Inn, SpringHill Suites, Fairfield or TownePlace near every convention center we've been to.

Step 2: Put your member number on every stay

Including the stay-to-play block. See my stay-to-play guide for how.

Step 3: Pay for the season with a card that earns in that program

Gym fees (if your gym takes cards without a big surcharge), gas, food on the road, and the hotel itself. The rule in our house: the card gets paid in full every month. Interest wipes out the value of points fast.

The card I use for all these hotel nights

Step 4: Spend points where they help most

  • The extra night before an early report time
  • Out-of-town events with no housing policy
  • Siblings' tournaments and the family trip after Worlds or Nationals

Award prices change with dates and demand, so I check the points price against the cash price before booking. If the cash rate is low, I pay cash and save the points.

Is it worth getting a hotel credit card for youth sports travel?

It can be if your family stays in hotels many weekends a year, sticks with one hotel program, and pays the card in full every month. If you carry a balance, interest will cost more than the points are worth.
